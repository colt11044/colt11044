## Research

- **Reducing Geographic Disparity in Kidney Allocation using a Redistricting Integer Program and Discrete Event Simulation** (U.S. Naval Academy, 2019) — My undergraduate research on kidney allocation geography, combining integer programming with simulation.

  **Abstract from the paper:**

  > Geographic disparity in kidney allocation leaves certain candidates at a large disadvantage due to their location within the United States. We generalize Professor Sommer Gentry’s liver optimized redistricting model for liver allocation and apply it to kidney allocation. The objective of the integer programming redistricting model is to minimize percent of demand not met in the worst-off/most disadvantaged district by dividing the country into a certain number of contiguous and compact districts. Then, we simulate distributing kidneys in the designed districts, using the Kidney-Pancreas Simulation and Allocation Model (KPSAM) to determine whether the new districts significantly reduce geographic disparity. Sharing kidneys in an optimized 25 district scheme reduces the difference in percent of demand not met between the best off and worst off districts. After simulation, the optimized model significantly reduces the variation in waiting times for both patients who have received a transplant and those still on the waiting list.
